# Lead design review: every dream, the astronaut now, and the build order

Advisor note, 2026-10-29. It is advice only and changes no code. The owner's words and `ROADMAP.md` set the bar; this note says how to clear it. All names are invented.

---

## 1. Design pillars: what every dream must deliver

1. **A career, not a theme park.** Each area is one real hardship of the job: the grind, the rejection, the rival, the cost, the doubt. The player feels it in their hands, not in a caption.
2. **Walk in, the world changes, one job opens the way out.** This is the clock-house rule. Each area has one goal you can see from the door, and doing it changes something you can see outside.
3. **One verb per dream, grown three times.** The verb is taught safely, tested under pressure, then mastered at the catch. The trumpet, the brush and the gears all follow this. No mini-game should appear only once.
4. **Every object says what it is and what it's for.** You can name it from its silhouette alone. It has one sign, in the world, next to it. If it's a threat, it moves like one before it hurts.
5. **A catch you earn and remember.** The last area is the career's one public moment: the stage, the critic's plate, the reading, the vault, the launch, the last hand, seven o'clock. It ends on a held image and one line.
6. **Fair, short, restartable.** A dream takes 6–10 minutes. You never lose more than about 40 seconds of progress, and you can always restart this room, this dream, or go home.

## 2. The dreams

The format for each dream: the real struggle, then the arc as areas (each with its one objective and its foes), the catch, and what to cut.

### 2.1 Clockmaker: THE STOPPED HOUR (rebuilt; the owner's favourite)

**The real struggle.**
- Years of apprenticeship spent on files and pivots before you are trusted with a movement.
- Precision that nobody sees, and eyes that go.
- Cheap factory clocks undercut the trade.
- Being noticed only when something fails.
- The town runs on your time and never learns your name.

**Critique.**
- The pattern is right: inside the house you fix the clock, and the green lights up outside.
- The dream is too thin. It has four rooms (lane, green, clock house, yard) and one encounter (three gears, one timing press). That is about 3 minutes, and the struggle is only told by the conductor.
- Nell's request and the catch in the bell yard have no friction between them.

**Fixes: grow it to six areas, keeping the loop where the inside opens the outside.**

| # | Area (walk in, it changes) | One objective, and the mechanic that expresses the struggle | Foes and obstacles |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | THE LANE at 6:00, lamps dead | Find the lamplighter. Then light the first lamp by hand-winding its timer, which teaches the gear-press verb safely. | None. Fog, and a cat on the wall as a guide. |
| 2 | THE WORKSHOP (Master Odile's, inside; north window, a hundred ticking clocks) | **Apprentice test:** file a pivot until it fits. The bench is a zoomed view and the press window narrows each pass. A miss costs time, never failure. Odile says "again". | The ticking itself: every clock in the room drifts, and the room hushes as you get it right. |
| 3 | THE GREEN, the town square | Carry the pivot across the green while the townsfolk stream home in the dark, bumping you. Protect a fragile item: if you're hit, you go back to the last lamp. | Hurrying townsfolk, a dog, puddles. Each lamp you've lit is a checkpoint. |
| 4 | THE CLOCK HOUSE, inside the tower: rooms of gears, a mezzanine | **The escapement:** seat three gears, then regulate the pendulum. Hold to swing, release on the beat, so the timing verb grows. When it starts, the green outside lights up. Keep this beat. | Swinging weights and turning wheels are the platforms and the hazards. |
| 5 | THE SHOPFRONT (a factory-clock salesman, Mr Pellow, sets up a stall of cheap clocks that all show different times) | The town has started setting its watches by his stall. Restart the town's agreement: three townsfolk come and check their watches against the tower, and you whistle them over (the existing whistle verb). | Mr Pellow's barker blocks you, and his clocks chime out of sync to drown the whistle. |
| 6 | THE BELL YARD, at 7:00 | **The catch:** strike seven by hand on the hammer, one press per beat, as the whole town looks up. The lamps light street by street. Nobody looks at Jo. Nell nods. | None. |

**Cut:** any dialogue that explains "nobody will know your name". The empty yard after the bell already says it.

### 2.2 Musician: THE BIG STAGE (rebuilt; the reference)

**The real struggle.**
- Playing to empty rooms, and busking for coins.
- Day jobs.
- Bandmates leaving.
- Being told to sound like someone else.
- Gear stolen or broken.
- Stage fright.
- The van and the road.

**Critique.**
- The best rebuild. Its six places and choice carry-over (Marcus, Tally) are the template.
- Gaps:
  - Busking, the road and the breath meter were lost, so the "struggle to be heard" part is now just a cellar.
  - The neon gig in the alley is a second tutorial before the first gig.
  - Marcus stands where he should sit.
  - The fire stairs are a nine-storey climb with no musical meaning.

**Fixes.**
1. **Replace the alley's neon gig with BUSKING on the street.** Play phrases while people walk past. A good phrase stops a passer-by and they drop a coin; misses make them walk on. The goal is "enough for the cellar's door fee". This teaches the verb and the struggle at once.
2. **Fire stairs become THE ROAD.** Each landing is a gig night: an empty bar, a wedding, a rainy festival tent. Each gives one card of Ray's advice. The climb now means years.
3. **The blank at the Big Stage is the best beat in the game.** Protect it, and frame it with one second of total silence.
4. Sit Marcus down.
5. Keep NERVE as the dream's only meter. Hide it outside gigs.

### 2.3 Painter: THE MASTER'S LADDER (rebuilt)

**The real struggle.**
- Unpaid apprenticeship: grinding, gesso, copying the master.
- Working in the master's hand while the master signs.
- The guild gate.
- Patrons who dictate materials and price.
- A celebrated rival.
- Women shut out: Bice is the heart of this dream.

**Critique.**
- The world and the ladder are right.
- One problem: the craft engine ("press on the gold mark, misses never fail") runs four times (grind, drapery, test piece, giornata). With no stakes and no change, it is the same QTE four times.
- Seven places plus the bottega revisit is over 10 minutes.

**Fixes.**
- **Vary the verb as it grows:**
  - grind is a rhythm hold
  - the drapery is tracing a line (follow the fold)
  - the test piece is done under watching eyes: when a consul looks, you must hold still
  - the giornata combines all three against the drying plaster
- **Give the contract a choice.** Corso pays for azurite or ultramarine; ultramarine costs your coins, and the consuls notice. Money becomes a real pressure.
- **Merge:**
  - fold the colour-seller into the street, as the shop's shelves you climb from the street
  - fold the guild hall's test into the piazza's loggia
  - the result is six areas
- **The catch:** keep the vault and Bice painting the face. End on her name, if Jo named her, cut into the plaster's edge.

### 2.6 Astronaut: THE QUIET ABOVE (legacy, to redesign)

**The real struggle.**
- Years of training to near-impossible standards.
- Being rejected and reapplying.
- A friend selected instead of you.
- Medical tests.
- Isolation, and family left on the ground.

**What to keep.** The bones are the best story in the legacy set:

- the separate "body stats" economy, with four meters in the HUD
- the flashcard quiz, a menu reading test

**Redesign, to six areas.**

### 2.8 The finale: THE LAST STOP / THE LONG EVENING

**The payoff.** Every caught dream sends one person to the Evening, at the place their career gave them:
- Nell lighting lamps
- the cellar trio playing on a porch
- Bice painting a shutter
- Chef Sallow cooking outside
- Wren reading on a bench
- Priya pointing out a star
- Lou dealing cards for matchsticks

The Evening must count these on screen ("four lamps lit of seven") and end on the ridge bench with every caught dream's person visible in the town below. Without that, the last stop is scenery.

## 3. The astronaut, now: "TRAIN IT UP: THE TRACK"

Room `p1_track`, `src/data/astronaut/rooms.js:28-48`; built by `buildBody()` in `src/scenes/AstronautScene.js:418-430`. The map is `astronautData.js:47-52`.

**1. "Why is there a stair here?"**
- **What it is:** the climb to the training track. `stairs(rows, GROUND, 31, 4, 'r')` writes four `S` tiles. `S` is the `stair` role, which is a **solid** block, not one of the walkable stair slopes (`legend.js:17,41`). So it is four 32 px (0.73 m) risers. It stops one tile short of the deck at row 31, so the last step is another riser. The mezzanine itself (`fill 31, 35–55`) is plain gym-floor tile: no lanes, no rail, no start line.
- **Why it fails:** it has the scale of a cliff and nothing says "track". The player sees a white stair to a ledge.
- **Fix:**
  - Replace it with `k/K` half-pitch walkable stairs that reach the deck exactly.
  - Draw the deck as a **running track**: a red-brown surface with two white lane lines and a handrail in front.
  - Paint a start/finish line with a chequered stripe.
  - Hang a lap board on the wall above it: "LAPS 0 / 3 · TIME 0:41 / 0:35".

**2. "What is this broken thing?"**
- **What it is:** the `lv3_poster` landmark (`parallax.js:902-908`). It is a navy rectangle with a white bar and a white triangle: a rocket with no fins, flame or scale. It sits on the mid parallax layer, so it slides against the floor and appears to stand under the ledge.
- **Why it fails:** it carries the Board quiz clue ("MERIDIAN LV-3 · THREE STAGES"), but its caption sits low and is hidden behind the deck. A white triangle reads as a spike, which is the game's hazard shape.
- **Fix:**
  - Hang it high on the back wall as a framed **poster**: a frame, a pin at each corner, a drawn three-stage rocket with fins, separations and a flame.
  - Put the caption above the deck line.
  - Lock it to the wall layer (scroll factor 1) so it can't drift.
  - Never use a bare white triangle anywhere but a hazard.

**3. "Why is this just a circle? No objective."**
- **What they are:** the medicine balls (`AstronautScene.js:425-429`): `add.circle(…, 12, 0x8a2a20)` with no texture, rotation, shadow or sound. They move `speed × 0.016` per frame, so speed depends on frame rate (`:1967`). A hit knocks Jo back and silently resets `t.phase` to `'home'`, so the lap is lost without a word. The goal is only "train it up: the track". Nothing says what a lap is, how many are needed, or that the balls matter.
- **Fix:**
  - Draw a **medicine ball**: a stitched leather sprite that rolls (rotation = distance / r) with a ground shadow and a thud on each bounce.
  - Show who rolls it: a candidate at the far end, with a wind-up.
  - Use `delta`, not a fixed step.
  - On a hit, say "lap lost: start line" at the line itself.
  - Better still, follow §2.6: one circuit and a stopwatch, where the balls cost time, not progress.

**Other problems on the screen.**
- **"…KE · talk to the desk / first / OPEN"** is gate g0's sign (`GateSigns.js`). It stands 2.5 tiles left of the gate, so it is cut off at the room seam. It is wrong: g0 needs `d0`, a talk with **Coach Adaeze**, and there is no desk. It also stays up at 55 % alpha after opening.
  - Fix the hint to "talk to Coach Adaeze".
  - Remove the sign (fade to 0) a second after it reads OPEN.
  - Clamp sign x inside the room that owns the gate.
- **The green dot in mid-air** is g0's status light (`buildGates`, `:280`). The gate tiles fade out on open, but the light and its bar stay, turned green, hanging over nothing.
  - Fade the light and bar with the tiles.
  - Better still, make gates real **doors** that slide up into a frame, with the light on the frame.
- **The HUD is heavy.** Up to five layers stack:
  - the region board title
  - the objective
  - the caption hint bar
  - the 4-line LUNGS/GRIP/LEGS/NERVE tool line
  - the visor text

  Fix it like this:
  - Drop the four meters in favour of the wall stopwatch, as in §2.6. If stats stay, show only the one this room trains, as a small pip row by the lap board in the world.
  - The region title appears for 2 s on entry, then goes.
  - Show the objective on entry and when it changes, then collapse it to one short line.
  - The visor is redundant with the objective. Cut it.

## 4. Cross-cutting fixes

- **Objective communication.** Every area states its job three ways:
  - a line on entry, at most 6 words, as a verb plus an object ("run 3 laps under 0:35")
  - a **world sign** at the challenge
  - a **visible result** outside the room when it's done
  - No objective may name a thing the player can't see. The astronaut's "desk" fails this; so does any "train it up".
- **Signposting.**
  - Gates are doors, not tinted tile columns. A door shows its requirement on the door itself (an icon per requirement plus a lamp).
  - The next way on is always the brightest thing on screen; collectible lights are dimmer than the exit.
- **Teaching.** Show the verb first in a room with no failure, then once under a little pressure, then use it. Teach with a card only for controls, at most one card per dream, and replace caption hint bars after first use.
- **Object readability.** Ban untextured primitives (`add.circle`, `add.rectangle`, bare `add.triangle`) for anything the player must interpret. Every interactive or harmful thing needs:
  - a silhouette you could name
  - motion before it can hurt (wind-up, roll, sway)
  - a sound
  - a sign within one screen

  Run a lint pass that greps for `add.circle(` and `add.triangle(` in scene code at gameplay depths and lists every hit for redraw.
- **HUD diet.** At rest the HUD holds:
  - hearts
  - coins
  - one objective line, collapsible

  Meters (O₂, NERVE, plaster) appear only while they matter, then hide. Control hints show on the first two uses and then live in the pause menu.
- **Pacing.** Each dream follows the same shape:
  - calm teach (1 min)
  - grind (2–3 min)
  - the low point, a quiet dark room (1 min)
  - the climb back (2 min)
  - the catch (1 min)

  Every rebuilt dream needs its low point marked; the musician's is the road, the painter's the guild gate.
- **Difficulty.** Fail states cost time, not progress. Mastery is rewarded with coins on the hard path (collectibles skill), never by gating the catch. Encounters slow down after two misses (the musician already does; make it a framework rule).
- **Checkpoints and restart.**
  - BaseLevel's pause menu already has "Restart from the flag" (`BaseLevel.js:818`), but the owner didn't find it. Show "Esc · menu" in the corner for the first 10 s of every dream.
  - Add **"Restart this room"**, which resets the room's state, and **"Start the dream over"**, with a confirm.
  - Put a lamp checkpoint at every room entrance. The save-flag look must be the platform lamp everywhere, lit when touched with a chime, never a flag or a coloured dot.
- **Jo's glow.** Replace the halo with rim light from the scene's key light (one side, warm or cool per place) and a contact shadow. Jo stays at 3:1 contrast or better without looking radioactive.
- **Music level.** Music sits about 6 dB under ambience when the player is idle in a room. It returns on encounter.
- **The hub's role.**
  - Between dreams, the station should show what you brought back: a lamp lit on the concourse per catch, the person from that dream waiting on a bench, and the departure board row flipping to CAUGHT.
  - Keep the hub short: arrival to the next train in under 40 s.
  - Use Ro's lines as the one-line reflection.
- **The finale's payoff.** See §2.8: every catch puts a person in the Evening, and the ending names them.
