# Painter dream: Renaissance advice

Advisor note. Invented names only; real history is the model. It fits the existing plan in the worktree doc `dream_painter_renaissance.md` (THE MASTER'S LADDER, city of Fioralba, Bice, Maestro Gualtiero, rival Anselmo Dori). Where this note disagrees, it says so.

## 1. The ladder of a painter's career (Italian city-states, 1400s)
- **Errand boy, age 8-12.** Sweeps, fetches eggs, runs to the colour-seller, minds the fire under the glue pot. Unpaid; parents sometimes paid the master. Learns the shop by being in it.
- **Apprentice (garzone), age 12-19, about 5-7 years, under a written contract.** Board and lodging, no wages. In order:
  - Grind pigments on a porphyry slab with a muller. Azurite and earths for hours; lapis only when trusted.
  - Boil rabbit-skin glue, lay gesso on poplar panels, sand it smooth (the "gesso grosso" then "sottile" coats).
  - Draw daily on small prepared boxwood tablets with silverpoint, then on paper. Copy the master's drawings (the workshop pattern book), never invent.
  - Temper egg tempera: yolk, a little water, cracked fresh each morning.
  - Burnish gold leaf over red bole with an agate or dog's tooth.
  - Prick cartoons and pounce charcoal through them to transfer a design to plaster.
- **Assistant (aiuto/discepolo), age 19-24.** Paints drapery, backgrounds, secondary figures in the master's hand. The master signs. Paid by the day or the piece.
- **Guild membership, age about 20-26.** Painters usually enrolled in the physicians' and apothecaries' guild, because they bought and sold pigments. Needs: finished apprenticeship, a sponsor, entry fee, often a test piece. Brings the right to open a shop, sign contracts and take apprentices.
- **The masterpiece.** Often a test piece, not always formal: a panel shown to the guild consuls. Frame it as "show the consuls you can be trusted with their wall".
- **Commissions and contracts.** A notary drew them up. They named the subject, the size, the deadline, the price and the payment in instalments. They specified materials: "ultramarine at four florins the ounce, not azurite, for the robe; gold leaf for the halo and the stars", and sometimes demanded the master's own hand for faces. Fines for lateness. Patrons: guilds, confraternities, a banking family, a monastery.
- **Game use:** the contract is a great HUD object. Show it as a scroll with "ultramarine x2, gold leaf x10" which become the finale's resource counts.

## 2. Areas: buildings, props, jobs, one objective each
- **Bottega (interior).**
  - Props: porphyry slab, shell dishes of colour, egg basket, glue pot, panels drying on racks, the master's pattern book, a hanging plaster cast of a hand, a charcoal brazier.
  - Jobs: master, apprentices, a boy turning the spit of glue.
  - Objective: grind and temper three colours in order (rhythmic hold, then release). Fun beat: timing mini-game on the muller.
- **Via dei Pittori (street) and the colour-seller (speziale).**
  - Street: arched shop mouths with open fronts and counters, shutters, washing lines, a cypress, a well.
  - Colour-seller: jars to the beams, scales, a mortar, a copper still, sulphur and lapis in sealed boxes.
  - Jobs: spice-and-pigment seller, a porter, a carpenter.
  - Objective: climb the jar shelves by ladder for the lapis; a rival apprentice and a pigeon block the way. Shelves and rolling ladders give the vertical traversal.
- **The guild hall (loggia and chamber).**
  - Props: the banners of the arts, a long table, the matriculation book, a dais, a brass measure set in the wall.
  - Jobs: notary, consuls, doorkeeper, inspectors.
  - Objective: hand in the test piece. The room is a stealth and timing gate; the inspectors' sight cones decide when Jo can cross the hall.
- **The piazza.**
  - Props: fountain, market stalls, loggia, church front, campanile, and the dome in timber centering with a treadwheel crane.
  - Jobs: porters on the treadwheel, stonecutters, a water seller, friars.
  - Objective: cross by the loggia roofs and washing lines to reach the three doors; optional silverpoint "studies" are the side quests.
- **Patron's palazzo.**
  - Props: arcaded courtyard, a gallery above, a strongbox, ledgers, a tapestry, a coat of arms.
  - Jobs: steward, secretary, banker, foreman.
  - Objective: reach the banker's study past the steward's men and read the contract (the pigments list). Climb the gallery, stairs and wall hangings.
- **The new chapel (scaffolds).**
  - Props: pole-and-beam scaffolds lashed with rope, ladders, plaster troughs, lime pits, candles, buckets of water, plumb lines, a tent of wet cloths.
  - Jobs: plasterer (intonacaco), mason, rival workshop crew.
  - Objective: follow the giornata. Plasterer lays one day's patch; paint it before it dries (see finale).
- Cut or merge if scope is tight: the street and colour-seller can be one level; the guild hall and palazzo can be one hall with two doors.

## 3. Finale options (modelled on real contests, all invented)
- **A. The bronze door panels.** Six masters submit a trial relief in a single city contest; judges are the consuls. Pro: strong in story. Con: a static scene, and metalwork needs a forge cast that is hard to platform.
- **B. The dome.** Master builders compete with a model and a plan for a vault with no timber centering. Pro: spectacular. Con: it is about engineering, not painting.
- **C. The chapel vault on scaffolds, one bay each, judged at the bell.** Pro: it is the one with a natural **timer** (the fresh plaster), natural **vertical platforming** (ladders, planks, lashed poles), a visible **rival** progress bar and **resources** drawn from the contract (ultramarine, gold leaf). It also pays off every earlier skill: grinding, gesso, gold, cartoon and pouncing.
- **D. Public trial panel judged by the guild.** Pro: simple and dramatic. Con: flat, no climb.
- **Recommendation: C.** Layer in D as the closing cutscene (the panels shown to the crowd and the consuls at the bell).

## 4. Sound, in synth terms
Reference pitch A=440 is fine; drones on D or G. Keep reverb off exteriors, add long 3-4 s tails inside the church.
- **Bottega (inside).**
  - Music: a lute (plucked triangle/saw with fast decay and a lowpass at about 2 kHz), a slow Dorian dance in D (D E F G A B C), 72 BPM, a pedal drone on D.
  - Ambience: a pestle and mortar, a rhythmic scrape (filtered noise bursts at 1.2 Hz), a hearth crackle, a muffled street through a low-passed noise bed.
- **Street (outside).**
  - Music: recorder lead (sine+breath noise, soft vibrato 5 Hz) over lute, Mixolydian in G (G A B C D E F), 100 BPM, a saltarello feel in 6/8.
  - Ambience: swallows (short high chirps in random bursts, 3-6 kHz), market chatter (bandpassed noise with vowel formants, in bursts), cart wheels, a hammer.
- **Colour-seller (inside).**
  - Music: sparse lute, 60 BPM; add a few sustained harmonic partials as a glassy pad.
  - Ambience: a bubbling alembic (low-passed noise with random pops), glass clinks, a scale chain, a wood creak.
- **Piazza (outside).**
  - Music: a shawm lead (reedy saw with a band-pass nasal formant at about 1.2 kHz) and a hand drum, a stately Dorian dance at 90 BPM.
  - Ambience: a fountain (steady white noise band-passed 1-5 kHz with a slow ripple), bells for the canonical hours (a bell patch with inharmonic partials, a strike every few minutes; the bell is also the time cue), a crowd murmur, swallows, a mason's chisel (a metallic tick every 0.4 s from the dome side).
- **Guild hall (inside).**
  - Music: a portative organ with a flute stop (drawbar-style sines), slow Dorian, 60 BPM, a stately one-note-at-a-time line.
  - Ambience: a stone echo (1.5 s), a quill scratch, footsteps on flagstones, a low murmur.
- **Palazzo (inside).**
  - Music: a lute and recorder duet, a Mixolydian pavane at 66 BPM; courtly and a little cold.
  - Ambience: a distant courtyard fountain, a clock-free silence (no mechanical ticking), a door latch.
- **Chapel (inside, climax).**
  - Music: a plainchant-like drone (D+A open fifth) with a slow organ pad; mode Dorian. As the contest runs, add a pulsing lute pattern, tempo rising 60 to 84 BPM.
  - Ambience: a very long echo (3-4 s), candle flicker, plaster scrape, a trowel tap, a rival's chisel on the far scaffold, pigeons flapping in the vault, and the **bell at the end**.
- General: no brass fanfares; no guitar or piano; no drum kit. A tambourine or frame drum only.

## 5. Anachronisms and clichés to avoid
- **Oil paint as the default.** Egg tempera is the base; oil is only just arriving from the north and is a luxury. Fresco is plaster only, not canvas.
- **Canvas and the easel painting on it.** Poplar panels, plaster walls. Easels were rare; use a trestle, a lectern, or a leaning panel.
- **The artist as lone bohemian genius.** They were craftsmen in guilds with contracts. Avoid the beret, the palette thumb-hole, the smock and the paint-tube.
- **Mona-Lisa-type smiles, vitruvian figures, ceiling-touching-fingers.** Don't copy famous compositions. Invent the subject.
- **Leonardo-style flying machines, or domed-hat cliches.** Skip mad-inventor gags.
- **Sans-serif signs, Arabic numerals on every sign, and a glass window of modern clarity.** Use Roman numerals on the contract and roundel glass.
- **Blue as cheap.** Ultramarine was costlier than gold; treat it as the precious thing.
- **Women painters presented as normal.** They were rare. Bice's story depends on that; don't casually add them to the guild.
- **Forks, potatoes, tomatoes, tobacco, coffee, tea, chocolate, maize.** All absent.
- **Gunpowder, printed books, spectacles on everyone.** Printing is only just arriving; most documents are handwritten.
- **Church-organ-as-only-music.** Secular life ran on lute, recorder, shawm and voices.
- **Whole-tone/chromatic "mystical" synths, minor-key-equals-sad.** Stay modal.
